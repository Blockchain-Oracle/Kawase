## Send

Say or type who and how much ("send 20 dollars to Alex", "pay @mira 5 euros"), or use the Send screen. You can send **any
token you hold on Arc**. Kawase:

1. **Finds the recipient**: a saved contact, a Kawase `@handle`, an `0x…` address you typed yourself, or a **QR code** you
   scanned. The agent can only use an address that came from your contacts, a code you scanned, or your own message, word
   for word.
2. **Picks the token** you hold, or asks if it is unclear.
3. **Checks the recipient on every payment**: a token contract or a look-alike of a saved contact is refused, a contract
   that isn't a wallet is warned about, the token issuer's blocklist is checked where it has one, and your own address is
   named as yours. It also checks your balance, your limits and whether a contact's address changed recently.
4. **Shows the payment card** with the exact amount, token, recipient and network.

Then one of two things happens:

| Situation | What you do |
|---|---|
| Inside what is left of today's allowance for that token, to someone you have paid before or named in this request | Nothing. It is sent at once and Kawase says so. |
| Above the allowance, a token outside it, a new address, or a look-alike address | Confirm with Face ID (fingerprint or screen lock on Android, an email code on the web). |

A payment to an address you just added can suggest sending a **test amount** first. A payment is only marked **paid** when the network's
receipt shows the exact transfer. If the answer is unclear, the card says "still checking" and Kawase keeps looking; it
never sends twice.

**Their wallet is on another chain?** The review offers it: USDC opens the [move card](./bridge-and-withdraw.mdx) with the
same amount and recipient; any other token offers converting to USDC first.

## Scan to pay

Tap **Scan** in the quick-action fan, on Send, or in chat's header. The scanner reads:

- a bare address, or a payment link with an amount and token (EIP-681): Send opens filled in, and the usual checks run;
- a Kawase request or invoice link: its pay page opens.

A code for the other Arc network, another chain or a closed request says why, with a network switch where that helps. You
can also paste a code instead.

## Receive and add money

Your wallet has **one address on both networks**, and it takes **any token on Arc**. **Add money** gathers every way in:

- **Buy** with a card ([Buy](./buy.mdx));
- **From an exchange**: the exchange's logo and its own steps (Coinbase through Base, then Bring to Arc);
- **From another chain**: USDC waiting at your address elsewhere, brought to Arc with no gas;
- **Receive**: your address and its QR code;
- **Test USDC** on testnet.

Money that arrives shows up in Activity and the inbox, can wake your [automations](./automations.mdx), and offers
**Save sender as a contact**.

## Request and split

"Request 15 dollars from Sam for dinner", or open Request. Kawase creates a **payment request** in any token on Arc with a
shareable link. The person paying can open it in any browser; the request is marked paid when a Kawase payment or a
matching plain transfer arrives.

**Split a bill**: "split 30 dollars for dinner with Amina and Tunde". Equal shares or by hand, with you included or not.
Each person gets their own request and link, and a progress card shows who has paid.

## Receipts and links

- **Share a receipt** of a finished payment as an image card (or text with the explorer link on the web).
- Every action has its own link (`/i/…`). Notifications open the exact card; a link opened while signed out comes back to
  it after sign-in. A card blocked by something you then fix (sign in, setup, limits, adding money) checks again by itself.
