"Buy 50 dollars of USDC", or **Add money → Buy with Apple Pay or card** (the first row).

<Steps>
<Step>
### Choose the amount
Pick a preset or type an amount, in dollars or euros. The sheet shows the payment methods Circle offers and says plainly
whether this is **real money** (mainnet) or **Circle's sandbox** (testnet, test cards only).
</Step>
<Step>
### Pay at Circle
**Buy** opens Circle's checkout full screen (where Apple Pay works). Kawase creates the session on its server, for **your
own address** on Arc.
</Step>
<Step>
### Funded
Back in Kawase, the order is followed until **the chain confirms the delivery**, then you see "You're funded". If the
payment didn't complete, Circle's reason is shown with **Try again**.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Buying another token

Ask for any other token ("buy 30 dollars of cirBTC with Apple Pay") and Kawase buys USDC first, then opens the swap for
what arrived, with one more approval and both fees shown. It only ever continues to a canonical or listed token, never a
look-alike.

## Where it runs

- **Mainnet**: Circle's production Onramp with real cards. Apple Pay and Google Pay appear once Circle finishes its
  business check of Kawase's domain; the checkout shows which methods it offers.
- **Testnet**: Circle's Onramp sandbox with test cards. No real money moves. The free
  [faucet](../getting-started.mdx#4-get-test-money) works too.

Selling to a bank account isn't built. To cash out, send to an exchange that accepts Arc deposits (see
[Withdraw without Kawase](../withdraw-without-kawase.mdx)).
