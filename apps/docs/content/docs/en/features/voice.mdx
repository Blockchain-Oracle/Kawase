Tap the orb in the dock and speak. The page blurs, the orb rises and listens; it shows the words it heard, answers aloud,
and puts the same **cards** on screen as chat does.

- **One conversation.** What you said by voice is in your chat thread, and typing picks it up where you left off.
- **Captions on both sides**: what Kawase heard, and what it is saying.
- **A spoken "yes" never approves a payment.** A send by voice is prepared on its card and waits for your tap and Face ID
  (fingerprint or screen lock on Android) — even inside your daily allowance.
- **Mute** with one tap: nothing is heard or sent while muted. **Type instead** is always there.
- **The orb shows the network**: lime on mainnet, sky blue on testnet.

## On the web

Voice works in the web app too: allow the microphone when the browser asks. If you refuse it, or there is no microphone,
Kawase stays connected, says how to allow it, and typing is right beside it. Kawase's own voice still plays.

## Settings → Voice

- **Kawase's voice**: one of six.
- **Speaking speed**: relaxed, normal or brisk.
- **Kawase speaks first**: it greets you as soon as voice opens; turned off, it waits for you.

Changes apply the next time you start talking.

## How it works

The phone or browser joins a LiveKit room over WebRTC, and Kawase's voice worker joins the same room. Every money or
account request is handed to **the same agent turn as chat** (same tools, same limits, same thread); the cards arrive on
your screen while the answer is spoken. Before speaking amounts, assets, @handles or the network, the worker checks them
against what the tools returned and corrects a mismatch. If the realtime voice can't start, Kawase falls back to a
listen → answer → speak pipeline with the same agent behind it. LiveKit doesn't record the conversation.
