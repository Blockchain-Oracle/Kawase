Kawase works on two networks: **Arc mainnet**, where money is real, and **Arc testnet**, where it is not. The **network
pill** at the top of Home switches between them in one tap, both ways, like dark mode.

## Always visible, never colour alone

- **The pill** names the network on Home, and in Settings → Network.
- **On testnet**, pushed screens carry a strip: **"Testnet · no real money"** (see [Send](./send-receive-request.mdx)).
- **On mainnet**, money screens carry a **Real money** chip:

<Shot src="network/mainnet-swap" alt="Swap on Arc mainnet with the Real money chip in its header." zoom="82 4.3 11">
  <Pin box="70 2 26 4.5">Real money</Pin>
</Shot>

## How it behaves

- **New accounts start on testnet**, and anything missing or unreadable falls back to testnet: the safe direction.
- **The first switch to mainnet** asks you to confirm that money there is real; after that it is instant. Guests can
  look around both networks; mainnet money needs an account.
- **Your wallet is the same** on both: one address, the same contacts and settings. Balances and activity are per
  network.
- **Drafts stay where they were made.** Something you're reviewing stays on the network it was made on; switch back to
  approve it.
- **The server decides.** The network is part of your session on the server, never chosen by the AI model; every
  payment carries the network it was prepared for, and a mismatch stops it.

When something exists on only one network (the faucet on testnet, long and short on mainnet), Kawase says so and offers
to switch rather than failing. See [networks](../networks.mdx) for the full comparison.
